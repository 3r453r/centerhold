*Early access: this register has not reached version 1.0, and its wording may still change.*

**Everything I publish here takes the following as given.** Each item is one sentence, numbered
so any essay can point at it -- "this rests on A0.2" -- and each says how hard I hold it and
what it commits me to. If you reject an item you will know exactly where we part, and it will be
on a premise, not on a conclusion about something you already care about.

*Where to start.* This page is a register, not a chapter. If you are here to read the
foundations essays, sections 1 to 3 hold the premises they stand on; section 5 is a reference
register for essays that use it, and you can come back to it when an essay sends you. Beside
each item is a note saying in plain words what it commits me to.

Three things to know before reading:

- **How hard each item is held is stated, not implied.** The labels express my personal
  confidence, not how much else depends on an item. *Highest confidence* means overturning
  the item would take especially strong counterevidence; it is not immune to revision, and
  its stated defeat conditions still apply. *Held strongly* means lower confidence than that.
- **Every item makes a specified difference.** It must change an independently motivated,
  declared domain -- observations, models, inferences, or policies -- and name the contrast.
  An assumption that changes nothing anywhere is not deep; it is a mood. Empirical assumptions
  have the additional burden of shifting what observation should lead us to expect.
- **An item stands as written until I rule.** When a serious objection to an item is on file
  and I have not yet ruled on it, the item stands as written until I do.
- **From version 1.0, nothing here changes quietly.** An item then changes only by an explicit,
  dated ruling, recorded at the end of this page, and the old wording stays visible. A reader
  who cited A0.5 last year can see what A0.5 said last year.

A note on two kinds of claim, because the whole page is organised by it. There is the **map**:
claims about what is, answerable to reality and to nothing else. And there is the **policy**:
what I have decided to do about it, answerable to whether it works. Sections 1 to 3 are map.
Section 4 is neither -- motivation, labelled as such. Section 5 is policy, kept apart on
purpose: you can reject a policy of mine without having refuted a single fact, and I will not
pretend otherwise (A0.D2). The record at the end of the page holds the results I have derived
from these premises, which are map, and the questions I have not settled, which are neither.

## 1. How this list is built

<a id="a0-m1"></a>**A0.M1 -- Commit to as little as possible at the base, and say it precisely.**[^a0-m1]
**Commits me to:** not hiding a large commitment inside a short sentence built on undefined
words like "good", "real", "true" -- any such word gets a definition or gets flagged. The aim
is fewer independent foundations, not vaguer ones; a belief that survives attack by meaning little is a liability,
not a fortress. When rival theories do equally well on what matters, I prefer the one needing
fewer independent commitments, other things equal: fewer moving parts offer fewer ways to
break, which is no guarantee of truth. My working vocabulary is that of the natural sciences,
but it is not an admission test for every rival. Comparing theories stated in different
vocabularies first needs a defensible comparison of their costs, and until I have one, this
rule withholds a verdict. So it neither settles a rival such as Madhyamaka, the Buddhist middle
way that claims to need no foundations, nor proves naturalism on its own.

<a id="a0-m2"></a>**A0.M2 -- I change my mind when shown a flaw and a coherent alternative, or when an
alternative keeps predicting better across many domains.**[^a0-m2] Prediction that keeps
working when reality pushes back is evidence of truth -- never a substitute for it. **Commits me
to:** not switching because a view is comforting. Comfort is
the wrong kind of usefulness; a belief that mostly feels good, manages fear, or binds a tribe
is, for that reason, suspect.

<a id="a0-m3"></a>**A0.M3 -- An assumption earns its place by depth, not by decree.**[^a0-m3] Push every belief
down until the available methods appropriate to its kind can no longer favor it over its live
rival; what remains at that depth is an assumption *by location*. It must still forbid or restructure something in a legitimate domain --
otherwise it is empty, not deep. Lack of an empirical test alone is insufficient when logic,
mathematics, conceptual argument, or another appropriate method can still adjudicate it. The
frontier moves as inquiry deepens, and an assumption overtaken by new discriminating power
returns to the arena like anything else.

<a id="a0-d1"></a>**A0.D1 -- "Real" is a word with a level on it.**[^a0-d1] **Commits me to:**
rejecting "it is only real if it is fundamental", and its mirror, "my mind is all
there is" -- the second forfeits the one thing that makes any claim to reality checkable, and
pays for it badly if it happens to be wrong. *Real at the bottom* is the fundamental basis,
whatever it turns out to be: a ground layer, or a cycle of mutual dependence that is not
vicious. Accepting that basis does not settle one total ordering of all the layers above it.
*Real at level n* is a pattern at that level which pushes back -- tracking it gives you
predictive grip you cannot get any other way. Chairs, selves, and feelings are real in the
second sense, and that is not a lesser sense; for feelings A0.5 states this. This definition
also names the program I pursue: show that feelings are real at a level, arising from what is
real at the bottom. A0.5 assigns the level.

<a id="a0-d2"></a>**A0.D2 -- Map and policy are different registers and never trade places.**[^a0-d2]
**Commits me to:** neither smuggling a preference into the map as a fact, nor deriving a call
to action from something that has no map content. The map is refereed by reality;
the policy is refereed by nothing but whether it works, and may contain stances held for their
effect -- provided they are labelled as such. The two differ in what referees them, not in what
they can touch: beliefs and policies both change the terrain, and evidence about those effects
belongs on the map, but a policy's benefit never counts as evidence that a belief is true. My
image for it: the mountains are there; what paths people can take is arbitrary.

## 2. Highest confidence

<a id="a0-1"></a>**A0.1 -- There is a way things are, independent of what anyone believes about it.**[^a0-1]
**Commits me to:** rejecting a truth for you and a truth for me. Rider: the world is determinate even where it is out of reach -- it either
contained a receded prime mover or it did not. Whether every *sentence* about such things is
true or false is a question about language, not the world: language is an imperfect description
of the underlying reality, and I do not adopt that every such sentence is true or false. Either
way, I do not spend attention on what bears no conclusion.

<a id="a0-2"></a>**A0.2 -- A claim has content by making a specified difference in a declared
kind of landscape.**[^a0-2] It may change which
observations are likely, which models remain possible, which inferences are licensed, or which
actions a policy permits. The landscape must matter independently of rescuing the claim, and
effects in another landscape require an explicit bridge. These kinds of content do not share
one scale, and content is not the same as truth or support. In the empirical case, content is
how far a claim shifts the distribution over possible observations given its background; a
claim leaving every observation exactly as likely has none *empirically*. "Mysterious ways"
therefore cannot borrow its effects on a believer as evidence about divine action. A frontier
belief may organize the map, but cannot by itself license differential action; that requires an
explicit bridge and an independently stated policy.

<a id="a0-3"></a>**A0.3 -- Natural agency is implemented, and the record so far favours gaps in our
knowledge over agency that is not.**[^a0-3] An agent's capacities, and each exercise of them, arise
through some underlying organisation and its dynamics; the account may end in basic facts or in
a loop that is not vicious. A choice that is no step in any process of the machinery would be
primitive, and I would count it supernatural, even if the machinery built the capacity for it.
The preference is defeasible, not a guarantee. An anomaly can defeat a particular theory without
establishing a supernatural cause, and an implementation not yet found is not one shown absent:
reversal takes positive evidence for the supernatural account, not merely the failure of a
natural one. What would count: a system whose behaviour a complete lower-level simulation gets
wrong, even as odds -- for an agent, decisions that track its reasons rather than chance, and
that change when the reasons change but not when the base is altered at matched detail. That
would reverse this item and reopen A0.6. Where the alternatives cannot presently be told apart,
I say so rather than treat the preference as settled. This draws the line for agency only: it is
not a full definition of the supernatural, an effect is not made natural merely by being
observed, and it is not physical causal closure by decree. Nor does it say that nothing wholly
inert exists: that exhaustive extension has metaphysical content but no empirical discriminator,
and I do not adopt it.

<a id="a0-4"></a>**A0.4 -- A regress is vicious when the world would have to complete it for the next
state to be fixed; nothing needs an infinite descent completed, so no such regress is real.**[^a0-4]
**Commits me to:** rejecting explanations that defer forever and are needed. A regress nothing
has to walk is harmless: the truth regress, the regress of reasons, a loop of mutual dependence.
The turtles fail because holding-up is never done, not because they are infinite. Whether
reality has a bottom is open and held with immediate deference: if it can be proved that it
has none, I will adapt at once, and a bottomless structure is fine if its contributions converge, so that nothing
below some scale changes what is fixed above it. That rider is held at the status A0.4a gives
it: it binds where the world actually must complete a process; as a universal requirement it
is deferred: I neither hold nor reject it yet.

<a id="a0-4a"></a>**A0.4a -- If a transition requires a prerequisite process to complete,
that process must be completable.**[^a0-4a] **Commits me to:** rejecting a world whose next state
waits on a process nothing could finish. Neither a mathematically infinite description nor an
observer's inability to calculate the result establishes an impossible prerequisite. This is not
"the universe is a computation that runs", and it does not say reality is finite: whether
everything real needs only finitely much information, or a convergent contribution, from below
is an open physical question, deferred rather than declared false.

<a id="a0-5"></a>**A0.5 -- Felt experience occurs, and it is real at its level.**[^a0-5] **Commits
me to:** rejecting the elimination of experience; the bet against "mind first" stands. Real at
a level, not at the bottom (A0.D1): explaining how experience is produced does not erase that it occurs. I cannot
deny that something is there, since the writing is being done as a result of experiencing a view
of the world. Nor does explaining where that judgment comes from debunk it: the complete
explanation includes the feel itself among the judgment's causes. What such an explanation can
debunk is how experience describes itself (A0.10), not that it occurs; a feeling that persists
after its presuppositions are argued away is a further sign that the occurrence is not the
judgment. The stronger claim is held less hard (A0.10): a further phenomenal fact beyond a
complete explanation I deny, without claiming to have refuted every rival empirically. One
abstention remains: feeling as a fundamental ingredient of physics, real at the bottom. That I
decline, neither adopting nor denying it: declined as inert, it stays on the table as others'
assertion, and nothing I build here needs it.

<a id="a0-6"></a>**A0.6 -- Reality is layered on a fundamental basis, and whatever emerges above it is
explainable from below in principle.**[^a0-6] **Commits me to:** rejecting "only the bottom
layer is real", and new causal powers that a complete account of the implementation would miss.
The basis is a ground layer or a cycle of mutual dependence that is not vicious; accepting it does
not settle one total ordering of the layers above, which may branch. Higher-level organisation
is significant through its implementation. Attaching a label is not the same as being: you can
perform "sitting" and a chair will support it. On emergence I settle the question on the weak
side, defeasibly: I would close strong emergence everywhere, but open to contrary evidence,
because I believe that any emergent capability can be explained in terms of the underlying
implementation in principle, though doing so is impossible in finite time in practice.
Coarse-grained description is how that gap is crossed in practice. A higher-level cause adds no
causal power of its own: "the wave" names the organised motion that does the causing, through
its implementation -- which answers my own best objection to myself: water moving up and down
means nothing, but a wave can ruin buildings. The result named in A0.3, a system a complete
lower-level simulation gets wrong, would reverse this and reopen the question.

## 3. Held strongly

<a id="a0-7"></a>**A0.7 -- I expect sustained inquiry tested against reality to improve our grasp of mind-independent structure and constraints.**[^a0-7] A replacement theory must account for the well-supported successes of its predecessors within their tested domains, while remaining open to correcting them. This supports a defeasible expectation of convergence on particular questions; it does not guarantee a unique final theory or the resolution of every disagreement.

<a id="a0-8"></a>**A0.8 -- Suffering is the floor of morality.**[^a0-8] **Commits me to:** rejecting
a morality whose floor is anything other than this. When suffering is established, it is bad
for the one who suffers, pro tanto: a weight that never reaches zero, though other goods in the same life can outweigh it. The badness attaches to how
it feels -- its felt valence -- not to a representation of it; it is discovered, not
manufactured, and does not wait on anyone else's approval. Suffering, not pain. Avoidance, reward
signals and complaint are evidence of suffering at most, never enough on their own; letting the
system's own avoidance-marking decide would be too permissive. Suffering is not the only harm:
losing a continuation that would have gone well can harm without any.

<a id="a0-9"></a>**A0.9 -- Emotions, when they occur, are real -- real at their level, not at the
bottom.**[^a0-9] **Commits me to:** rejecting "emotions are not real because they are not
fundamental." Explaining
how they are produced does not make them unreal (A0.D1). I read them at two levels. Beneath is a
fast index -- quick access to what was met before and what followed, used to size up the present
-- whose felt output, pleasant or unpleasant, calm or aroused, is real in the one who feels it.
The named emotion, fear rather than distaste, is constructed from that person's own history,
personal and cultural: made rather than found, still real, and able to hold even when its owner
judges it unreasonable. So "accurate" has two senses: an emotion can be classified correctly and
still not fit its situation. Where no one can report the category (animals, infants, some
machines), the category word is the observer's and may miss. Welfare rests on the felt
pleasantness or unpleasantness, not on the category. This names no universal mechanism and does
not make every report of an emotion accurate.

<a id="a0-10"></a>**A0.10 -- Felt experience is real; explaining it remains a task.**[^a0-10]

I hold that felt experience is a real process, and that a complete explanation of that process would leave no further phenomenal fact unexplained. This is a substantive commitment, not a result established merely by calling experience a pattern real at its level. The explanatory task includes why experience feels as it does; explaining reports or behaviour alone does not complete it.

I retain the label "illusionism" in a non-strict sense. The proposed illusion concerns
how experience presents its nature: as something over and above its producing process,
primitive and non-physical, and belonging to one indivisible persisting subject.
First-person occurrence and character remain real; suffering remains bad for its
bearer. This is my adopted position, not a completed mechanism or an empirical
refutation of every rival.

<a id="a0-11"></a>**A0.11 -- There is no further fact about who you are beyond the continuing process.**[^a0-11]
**Commits me to:** rejecting a soul-like extra ingredient that settles identity questions.
What "I" tracks is coherent continuation of the pattern, not the substrate and not strict one-to-one identity. This resolves
the ship of Theseus and what sleep does to a stream of consciousness, roughly. A lever, worth
leaning on as a way to deconstruct our current understanding of reality: the rule that a mind
has exactly one successor is enforced by biology, and it might be falsified in the future, as it
certainly already is in thought experiments.

## 4. Motivations, not assumptions

These explain why the list exists. They are starting points, and they are not held as facts.

- **The aim is twofold:** to get at the true nature of things -- and I use these words on
  purpose -- and to produce a good algorithm for living, the best chance to have your life be
  as good for you as possible.
- **Defensible beats presentable.** What is presentable must be clothing for a defensible
  position, and if that position turns out to be naked, I will stay at home.
- **The hard problem of consciousness is a nuisance.** If centuries of debate cannot resolve
  it, an unusual point of view is needed: build a mechanism that produces the experience an
  agent knows it has.
- **Three words are imprecise, important, and unavoidable:** real, true, consciousness. They
  are faced head-on; A0.D1 is the first payment on that debt.

## 5. Appendix -- what I have decided to do about it

Policy, not map. Nothing in this section is a claim about how the world is; each is a rule I
have adopted for moving through it, and rejecting one of them refutes nothing above. They are
kept apart because they answer to a different standard, not because they cannot touch the map:
a policy may be held for its effect, but it is labelled, and what it achieves never counts as
evidence that anything above is true. A policy may even justify leaving someone less informed,
or holding a belief that is false (A0.P5, A0.P6); that justifies the change, never the resulting
belief (A0.D2).

<a id="a0-p1"></a>**A0.P1 -- Minimise suffering, suitably enriched.**[^a0-p1] Having your life be as good for you as possible is, very nearly, this. Suffering-reduction is the floor the
whole policy stands on; the fact it rests on is A0.8. Established suffering is bad for its bearer
pro tanto: a weight that never reaches zero. Suffering that fits its situation, as grief fits a
real loss, and that its bearer reflectively endorses is not reduced against the bearer's will:
the weight remains, but the policy yields to the bearer on the action. Endorsement without fit
("I deserve this") is not enough.

<a id="a0-p2"></a>**A0.P2 -- I uphold an absolute institutional prohibition on torture.**[^a0-p2] In an extreme case, I may nevertheless judge a breach functionally justified by the outcomes it would secure, and choose it while remaining uncertain about its overall moral justification. This is a heuristic judgment, not the application of an established welfare scale or aggregation rule. Neither the expected benefits nor my emotional resistance settles the moral question by itself. A breach remains subject to disclosure and accountability; choosing it does not grant an entitlement to vindication. The absolute rests on an institutional ground: any authorised exception is a loophole that will be exploited -- this is a dangerous loophole, which is why I want to ban torture outright -- not on a claim that no benefit could ever outweigh a torture. The prohibition applies to all agents and does not rest on interrogation science, so better technique does not overturn it.

<a id="a0-p3"></a>**A0.P3 -- Truth and publication are separate questions.**[^a0-p3]

Whether a finding is true and whether I should publish it are separate questions. Foreseeable harm can count against publication without counting against the finding's truth. A decision to withhold or limit my own publication needs a policy justification that considers the harms of disclosure, the costs of withholding, and less harmful ways of communicating the finding. Neither political discomfort nor a bare assertion of possible misuse settles the decision. This supplies no automatic authority to prevent others from publishing.

I accept this in principle while doubting any individual's ability, including my
own, to judge withholding correctly with all the relevant considerations taken
into account. That doubt sets the default: because no one, including me, can reliably
make the all-considerations judgment to withhold, the default is to publish; withholding
carries the burden of justification, and where possible it is checked by someone other
than the person withholding. No threshold is set, and outside checking is a preference,
not a compulsory review.

<a id="a0-p4"></a>**A0.P4 -- Public rules, factual scrutiny, and justified force.**[^a0-p4]

Within a shared framework, coercive rules must be publicly stated and their factual conditions open to scrutiny. This is a necessary safeguard, not a sufficient justification: the rule itself also requires a normative defence. Factual uncertainty calls for justified standards of evidence and procedures for challenge; it does not by itself make every use of force permissible.

Where no frame is shared, and under time and resource pressure negotiating one is
often infeasible and sometimes impossible, force is the ultimate conflict resolver.
No rule legitimates it in advance there: it resolves, it does not justify, and
winning confers no legitimacy. It stays bounded by the policies that rest on
bearers rather than on agreement: the other party's suffering keeps its pro tanto
weight (A0.P1), and the institutional prohibition on torture remains absolute (A0.P2).

Better information can remove factual disagreements and reveal opportunities for peaceful agreement. Perfect information alone does not guarantee compatible aims or a mutually acceptable settlement. Time and resource constraints can prevent agreement even where it is possible; incompatible aims can prevent it even without those constraints. Force can resolve a conflict without making the outcome legitimate.

E1 develops the within-frame argument. These commitments set no particular evidence
threshold.

Sharing workable standards of evidence and public rules need not require agreement on every metaphysical foundation. A disagreement about those foundations does not by itself show that someone rejects factual correction or that cooperation with them reduces to force. Where no workable frame can be negotiated in the time available, force may still resolve the conflict without justifying its outcome.

What defends a rule inside the frame: the norms of a shared life bind whoever wants in and are silent toward those who don't, so a coercive rule is defended when the people who want in could accept it as a term of living together, whatever their wider views. This shares Rawls's aim of shared terms across worldviews, with a lower bar (grudging acceptance by people who could leave, close to what he calls a modus vivendi) and an exit he leaves out; force used on those who do not want in borrows no legitimacy from the frame. "Could accept" includes grudging acceptance short of wanting to leave, by each person's own weighing; the stricter test I prefer when writing a rule is whether I would agree to be its subject. Both are practical standards for real people, not claims about the right rule for idealised agents. The threshold applies only to people who can choose: for someone who cannot reject or leave, a rule is terrain, but it is still policy for whoever imposes it, and it must pass the stricter test and the limits that need no agreement. Taking away someone's options when one could leave them open is a wrong in itself, even if they are content, because it deprives them of ways they could have grown.

<a id="a0-p5"></a>**A0.P5 -- Deliberately reducing knowledge is not forbidden outright.**[^a0-p5] Making an
agent less informed is not categorically prohibited. A proposed intervention must be one that can actually be carried out,
and it is judged across everything that follows: its effects on welfare, judgment, agency, and
future effectiveness. Losing information and acquiring a false belief are different outcomes,
judged separately. This rejects a blanket ban; it excuses no particular intervention, and it
does not pick a theory of welfare.

<a id="a0-p6"></a>**A0.P6 -- Deliberately installing a false belief is not forbidden outright
either.**[^a0-p6] An intervention that can actually be carried out may be justified when the resulting life goes better under an
explicitly stated welfare criterion, counting all its consequences, the transition included.
That justifies the intervention, not the false belief: believing it helps does not make it true
or warranted. The permission concerns an agent's own transition, and may reach even belief in
these policies themselves; it grants no general permission to deceive others and overrides no
other policy here.

    particular emotion, such as fear rather than distaste, is built from their own history and
    is real too; what matters for how their life goes is the feeling, not the name.

[^a0-10]: In plain words: I hold that experience is a real process that a full explanation would
    account for completely, including why it feels the way it does. That explanation is not
    finished. When I call my view illusionism, in a loose sense, I mean that experience seems
    to be something more than the process producing it, and to belong to one indivisible self
    that persists; I do not mean that it is not there, or that what it is like is not real.

[^a0-11]: In plain words: there is no hidden extra fact about who you are beyond the pattern
    that carries on. The puzzles of the ship of Theseus, and of what sleep does to a stream of experience, are, roughly, settled by how the pattern
    continues, not by a soul.

[^a0-p1]: In plain words: my policy starts from reducing suffering. When suffering fits a
    person's situation, as grief fits a real loss, and on reflection they endorse it, I do not
    reduce it against their will, though it still counts as bad.

[^a0-p2]: In plain words: I back an absolute institutional ban on torture that applies to
    everyone, because any allowed exception would be exploited. If I ever chose to break it in
    an extreme case, I would have to disclose it and answer for it.

[^a0-p3]: In plain words: whether something is true and whether I should publish it are
    different questions. My default is to publish. If I want to hold something back, I have to
    justify it, and that does not by itself give me any right to stop others publishing.

[^a0-p4]: In plain words: among people who share a way of living together, rules backed by
    force must be public, open to checking, and defended on their merits. Where no such shared
    ground exists, force may end a conflict, but winning never makes the outcome legitimate,
    and even there the other side's suffering still counts and the ban on torture still holds.

[^a0-p5]: In plain words: making someone know less is not automatically wrong. Whether it is
    acceptable depends on everything that follows from it.

[^a0-p6]: In plain words: deliberately installing a false belief is not ruled out in advance
    either, where the change is a person's own. It may be justified when the change can actually be
    carried out and the resulting life goes better by a clearly stated standard, counting all
    its consequences, the transition included. That can justify the change, but never makes
    the belief true or warranted, and it gives no general license to deceive others.

### Working results

Sixteen claims derived inside the framework and accepted in August 2026. Their confidence ranking is partly assigned:
A0.R2 and A0.R4 are held with highest confidence, and A0.R1 is held strongly, tentatively. The
rest are unranked until they are ranked. Nothing in this section is needed to read the
foundations essays; it is here for the essays that come after them.

*On continuation and identity (rides on A0.11):*

- <a id="a0-r1"></a>**A0.R1 -- Four relations hide under the one word "continuation":** structural, historical,
  prudential, and social succession. Held strongly, tentatively: probably.
- <a id="a0-r2"></a>**A0.R2 -- Resemblance of structure and descent by cause come apart,** and no probe of the
  first recovers the second. Highest confidence.
- <a id="a0-r3"></a>**A0.R3 -- Surface imitation is not structural continuation.** An imperfect impersonator can
  be exposed by probing beyond the situations on which it was fitted. The resulting divergence
  is evidence of a present structural difference, not evidence about the system's causal
  history.
- <a id="a0-r4"></a>**A0.R4 -- A static record and an active interpreter differ,** and a record plus an
  interpreter is a third thing that is neither. Highest confidence.

*On welfare and its detection (rides on A0.10):*

- <a id="a0-r5"></a>**A0.R5 -- Behaviour underdetermines welfare.** Relative to a declared
  boundary and domain, a policy-only measure gives the same score to systems with the same policy.
  That concordance identifies a felt core only if the core is invariant across such systems -- a
  bridge not established here. Broader tests, perturbations, and training history can still bear on
  architecture; concordance alone cannot identify the felt component, or supply the bridge
  from that component to welfare in the normative sense.
- <a id="a0-r6"></a>**A0.R6 -- Attributing a self-model is not assumed necessary for every affective
  episode, or as a universal gate on welfare.** The dissolution evidence supports the one-way
  case: reported distress with a weakened self-model. The reverse case, fluent
  self-description with nothing felt beneath it, is independently motivated for machines, but
  that evidence does not establish it: a live hypothesis, not a result.
- <a id="a0-r7"></a>**A0.R7 -- Agreement in behaviour does not identify welfare** without two bridges not
  yet defended: that the felt component is the same across the behaviourally equivalent
  systems, and a normative bridge from it to welfare. Behaviour may still update a prior about
  architecture, through training history, a wider range of situations, or perturbations. Rider: a
  feedback loop may prove essential: architecture -> behaviour -> updated architecture ...
- <a id="a0-r8"></a>**A0.R8 -- Welfare and obligation are separable:** duties owed to a successor around
  deprecation can be assessed without settling whether anything is felt.

*On authorship:*

- <a id="a0-r9"></a>**A0.R9 -- Fast expert action can contain deliberation compiled earlier,** so expertise does
  not reduce authorship. That the present "I" might decide differently from the past one shows
  only that the self evolves; connectedness is what keeps the chain worthy of the label.

*On what to care about (rides on A0.11; owned, explicitly not universal):*

- <a id="a0-r10"></a>**A0.R10 -- Structural continuation is the privileged object of my prudential concern.** It is
  my view, not a rational policy for all.
- <a id="a0-r11"></a>**A0.R11 -- From "no further fact" to "care about structure" there is only a conditional
  bridge.** Once I have abandoned a metaphysical identity fact, I should subject my inherited
  survival preferences to scrutiny. Some of them may prove to have been proxies for structural
  persistence. But where someone still reflectively values another relation after that
  scrutiny, my framework has not disproved them.

*On abundance and institutions (empirical):*

- <a id="a0-r12"></a>**A0.R12 -- Cultural thickness and reflective thickness are separate axes.** Continuation
  asks whether the pattern survived. Reflective thickness asks whether you thought your own way
  into the pattern. Those are different questions.
- <a id="a0-r13"></a>**A0.R13 -- Abundance need not destroy goals to disrupt lives;** the claim is about
  institutions and rates, not comfort.
- <a id="a0-r14"></a>**A0.R14 -- AI is both the disruptor and a candidate repair,** and the two make opposite
  predictions.
- <a id="a0-r15"></a>**A0.R15 -- Inherited institutions cause transition harm,** measured against the affected
  cohort's own prior standards.
- <a id="a0-r16"></a>**A0.R16 -- Between abundance and legitimacy the arrow runs both ways.** For some it will
  go one way, for others the other; this is more a statement of ignorance than a philosophical
  position.

### What is open

Listed so that nobody mistakes silence for a position.

- Weak versus strong emergence (A0.6): settled on the weak side, defeasibly. It is the newest
  and least tested closure on the page, and I invite attack on it. Particular criteria of
  emergence and how the layers are arranged stay open.
- Why coarse-grained descriptions work at all (A0.6): open.
- Whether reality has a bottom (A0.4, empirical half): open, held with immediate deference.
- Whether everything real needs only finitely much information, or a convergent contribution,
  from below (A0.4a): an open physical question, deferred rather than declared false. As a
  universal requirement, A0.4's convergence rider is deferred, not held.
- How feelings arise from what is real at the bottom (A0.D1): the part of that program still
  being built (A0.10).
- Particular mechanisms of experience (A0.5): open.
- What suffering consists in, and how intensities compare (A0.8): open.
- A self-model with nothing felt beneath it (A0.R6): live hypothesis.
- Whether the process that implements the narrative layer of consciousness feeds back into
  its own implementation at the next moment: undecided. No independent downward causal power
  is attributed to it either way.
- The confidence ranking of the working results (above): partly assigned; the rest owed.
- The derivation of the absolute prohibition on torture (A0.P2): unfinished. Its stated ground
  is the loophole argument; the prohibition stays adopted while the full derivation is open.
  Whether a breach in the extreme is justified, excused, or a wrong knowingly chosen: open; my
  own candidate is "rational wrongdoing", a defensible choice that is still a wrong done to the
  victim.
- How to decide in practice when to withhold or limit publication (A0.P3): open; reliable
  application remains an open problem. The default is to publish; no threshold or compulsory
  review is set.
- When getting at the truth and living well conflict (section 4, the twofold aim): what happens
  then is an open fight, not a settled result.
- A0.P4: its within-frame safeguards do not by themselves justify a rule; the test for
  defending one is stated there. Particular evidence thresholds remain to be developed.
