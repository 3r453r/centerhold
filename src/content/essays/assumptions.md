**Everything I publish here takes the following as given.** Each item is one sentence, numbered
so any essay can point at it -- "this rests on A0.2" -- and each says how hard it is held and
what it rules out. If you reject an item you will know exactly where we part, and it will be
on a premise, not on a conclusion about something you already care about.

*Where to start.* This page is a register, not a chapter. If you are here to read the
foundations essays, sections 1 to 3 hold the premises they stand on; sections 4 and 7 are
reference registers for essays that use them, and you can come back for those when an essay
sends you.

Three things to know before reading:

- **How hard each item is held is stated, not implied.** The labels express my personal
  confidence, not how much else depends on an item. *Highest confidence* means overturning
  the item would take especially strong counterevidence; it is not immune to revision, and
  its stated defeat conditions still apply. *Held strongly* means lower confidence than that. *Working result* means derived
  and accepted, with its confidence stated where I have assigned one. *Open* means I have not
  decided and say so.
- **Every item makes a specified difference.** It must change an independently motivated,
  declared domain -- observations, models, inferences, or policies -- and name the contrast.
  An assumption that changes nothing anywhere is not deep; it is a mood. Empirical assumptions
  have the additional burden of shifting what observation should lead us to expect.
- **Nothing here changes quietly.** An item changes only by an explicit, dated ruling, recorded
  in section 8, and the old wording stays visible. A reader who cited A0.5 last year can see
  what A0.5 said last year.

A note on two kinds of claim, because the whole page is organised by it. There is the **map**:
claims about what is, answerable to reality and to nothing else. And there is the **policy**:
what I have decided to do about it, answerable to whether it works. Sections 1 to 4 are map.
Sections 5 and 6 are neither -- motivation and open questions, labelled as such. Section 7 is
policy, kept apart on purpose: you can reject a policy of mine without having
refuted a single fact, and I will not pretend otherwise. [D2]

## 1. How this list is built

<a id="a0-m1"></a>**A0.M1 -- Commit to as little as possible at the base, and say it precisely.** The aim is
fewer independent foundations, not vaguer ones; a belief that survives attack by meaning
little is a liability, not a fortress. When rival theories do equally well on what matters, I
prefer the one needing fewer independent commitments, other things equal: fewer moving parts
offer fewer ways to break, which is no guarantee of truth. My working vocabulary is that of
the natural sciences, but it is not an admission test for every rival. Comparing theories
stated in different vocabularies first needs a defensible comparison of their costs, and until
I have one, this rule withholds a verdict. So it neither settles a rival such as Madhyamaka,
the Buddhist middle way that claims to need no foundations, nor proves naturalism on its own.
Rules out: hiding a large commitment inside a short
sentence built on undefined words like "good", "real", "true" -- any such word gets a
definition or gets flagged. [M0]

<a id="a0-m2"></a>**A0.M2 -- I change my mind when shown a flaw and a coherent alternative, or when an
alternative keeps predicting better across many domains.** Prediction that keeps working when
reality pushes back is evidence of truth -- never a substitute for it. Rules out: switching
because a view is comforting. Comfort is the wrong kind of usefulness; a belief that mostly
feels good, manages fear, or binds a tribe is, for that reason, suspect. [M1]

<a id="a0-m3"></a>**A0.M3 -- An assumption earns its place by depth, not by decree.** Push every belief down
until the available methods appropriate to its kind can no longer favor it over its live rival;
what remains at that depth is an assumption *by location*. It must still forbid or restructure
something in a legitimate domain -- otherwise it is empty, not deep. Lack of an empirical test
alone is insufficient when logic, mathematics, conceptual argument, or another appropriate
method can still adjudicate it. The frontier moves as inquiry deepens, and an assumption
overtaken by new discriminating power returns to the arena like anything else. [M2]

<a id="a0-d1"></a>**A0.D1 -- "Real" is a word with a level on it.** *Real at the bottom* is the fundamental
basis, whatever it turns out to be: a ground layer, or a cycle of mutual dependence that is not
vicious. Accepting that basis does not settle one total ordering of all the layers above it.
*Real at level n* is a pattern at that level which pushes back -- tracking it gives you
predictive grip you cannot get any other way. Chairs, selves, and feelings are real in the
second sense, and that is not a lesser sense; for feelings this is now ruled (A0.5). Rules
out: "it is only real if it is fundamental", and its mirror, "my mind is all there is" -- the
second forfeits the one thing that makes any claim to reality checkable, and pays for it badly
if it happens to be wrong. This definition also named the program I set out on: show that
feelings are real at a level, arising from what is real at the bottom. The level is now
assigned; how feelings arise is the part still being built (A0.10). [D1]

<a id="a0-d2"></a>**A0.D2 -- Map and policy are different registers and never trade places.** The map is refereed
by reality; the policy is refereed by nothing but whether it works, and may contain stances held
for their effect -- provided they are labelled as such. The two differ in what referees them,
not in what they can touch: beliefs and policies both change the terrain, and evidence about
those effects belongs on the map, but a policy's benefit never counts as evidence that a belief
is true. Rules out: smuggling a preference into
the map as a fact, or deriving a call to action from something that has no map content. My image for
it: the mountains are there; what paths people can take is arbitrary. [D2]

## 2. Highest confidence

<a id="a0-1"></a>**A0.1 -- There is a way things are, independent of what anyone believes about it.** Rules out:
a truth for you and a truth for me. Rider: the world is determinate even where it is out of
reach -- it either contained a receded prime mover or it did not. Whether every *sentence*
about such things is true or false is a question about language, not the world: language is
an imperfect description of the underlying reality, and I do not adopt that every such
sentence is true or false. Either way, I do not spend attention on what bears no conclusion.
[B-TRUTH-EXISTS]

<a id="a0-2"></a>**A0.2 -- A claim has content by making a specified difference in a declared
kind of landscape.** It may change which observations are likely, which models remain possible,
which inferences are licensed, or which actions a policy permits. The landscape must matter
independently of rescuing the claim, and effects in another landscape require an explicit
bridge. These kinds of content do not share one scale, and content is not the same as truth or
support. In the empirical case, content is how far a claim shifts the distribution over
possible observations given its background; a claim leaving every observation exactly as
likely has none *empirically*. "Mysterious ways" therefore cannot borrow its effects on a
believer as evidence about divine action. A frontier belief may organize the map, but cannot by
itself license differential action; that requires an explicit bridge and an independently
stated policy. [B-DISCRIMINATION, M3]

<a id="a0-3"></a>**A0.3 -- Natural agency is implemented, and the record so far favours gaps in our
knowledge over agency that is not.** An agent's capacities, and each exercise of them, arise
through some underlying organisation and its dynamics; the account may end in basic facts or in
a loop that is not vicious. A choice that is no step in any process of the machinery would be
primitive, and I would count it supernatural, even if the machinery built the capacity for it.
The preference is defeasible, not a guarantee. An anomaly can defeat a particular theory without
establishing a supernatural cause, and an implementation not yet found is not one shown absent:
reversal takes positive evidence for the supernatural account, not merely the failure of a
natural one. What would count: a system whose behaviour a complete lower-level simulation gets
wrong, even as odds -- for an agent, decisions that track its reasons rather than chance, and
that change when the reasons change but not when the base is altered at matched detail. That
would reverse this item and reopen A0.6. Where the alternatives cannot presently be told apart,
I say so rather than treat the preference as settled. This draws the line for agency only: it is
not a full definition of the supernatural, an effect is not made natural merely by being
observed, and it is not physical causal closure by decree. Nor does it say that nothing wholly
inert exists: that exhaustive extension has metaphysical content but no empirical discriminator,
and I do not adopt it. [B-NATURALISM, B-NATURALISM-EXHAUSTIVE]

<a id="a0-4"></a>**A0.4 -- A regress is vicious when the world would have to complete it for the next
state to be fixed; nothing needs an infinite descent completed, so no such regress is real.** A
regress nothing has to walk is harmless: the truth regress, the regress of reasons, a loop of
mutual dependence. The turtles fail because holding-up is never done, not because they are
infinite. Whether reality has a bottom is open and held with immediate deference: "if we can
prove reality has no bottom I will adapt immediately," and a bottomless structure is fine if its
contributions converge, so that nothing below some scale changes what is fixed above it. That
rider is held at the status A0.4a gives it: it binds where the world actually must complete a
process; as a universal requirement it is deferred, not held. Rules out: explanations that defer
forever and are needed. [B-NOREGRESS, B-IMPLEMENTED]

<a id="a0-4a"></a>**A0.4a -- If a transition requires a prerequisite process to complete,
that process must be completable.** Neither a mathematically infinite description nor an
observer's inability to calculate the result establishes an impossible prerequisite. This is
not "the universe is a computation that runs", and it does not say reality is finite: whether
everything real needs only finitely much information, or a convergent contribution, from below
is an open physical question, deferred rather than declared false. Rules out: a world whose
next state waits on a process nothing could finish. [B-IMPLEMENTED]

<a id="a0-5"></a>**A0.5 -- Felt experience occurs, and it is real at its level.** Real at a level, not at
the bottom (A0.D1): explaining how experience is produced does not erase that it occurs. I cannot
deny that something is there, since the writing is being done as a result of experiencing a view
of the world. Nor does explaining where that judgment comes from debunk it: the complete
explanation includes the feel itself among the judgment's causes. What such an explanation can
debunk is how experience describes itself (A0.10), not that it occurs; a feeling that persists
after its presuppositions are argued away is a further sign that the occurrence is not the
judgment. The stronger claim is held less hard (A0.10): a further phenomenal fact beyond a
complete explanation I deny, without claiming to have refuted every rival empirically.
Particular mechanisms are open. Rules out: eliminating experience; the bet against "mind first"
stands. One abstention remains: feeling as a fundamental ingredient of physics, real at the
bottom. That I decline, neither adopting nor denying it: declined as inert, it stays on the
table as others' assertion, and nothing I build here needs it. [B-EXPERIENCE]

<a id="a0-6"></a>**A0.6 -- Reality is layered on a fundamental basis, and whatever emerges above it is
explainable from below in principle.** The basis is a ground layer or a cycle of mutual
dependence that is not vicious; accepting it does not settle one total ordering of the layers
above, which may branch. Higher-level organisation is significant through its implementation.
Attaching a label is not the same as being: you can perform "sitting" and a chair will support
it. On emergence I have closed the question on the weak side, defeasibly -- "i'd close strong
emergence everywhere. but open to contrary evidence" -- because "it is my belief that any
emergent capability can be explained in terms of the underlying implementation in principle,
but impossible in finite time in practice." Coarse-grained description is how that gap is
crossed in practice; why it works at all is open. A higher-level cause adds no causal power of
its own: "the wave" names the organised motion that does the causing, through its
implementation -- which answers my own best objection to myself, kept on the record: "water
moving up and down means nothing, but a wave can ruin buildings." The result named in A0.3, a
system a complete lower-level simulation gets wrong, would reverse this and reopen the
question. This is the most recent closure on this page and the least tested, and I invite
attack on it. Rules out: "only the
bottom layer is real", and new causal powers that a complete account of the implementation
would miss. [B-LAYERS, B-NATURALISM]

## 3. Held strongly

<a id="a0-7"></a>**A0.7 -- I expect sustained inquiry tested against reality to improve our grasp of mind-independent structure and constraints.** A replacement theory must account for the well-supported successes of its predecessors within their tested domains, while remaining open to correcting them. This supports a defeasible expectation of convergence on particular questions; it does not guarantee a unique final theory or the resolution of every disagreement. [B-TRUTH-CONVERGES]

<a id="a0-8"></a>**A0.8 -- Suffering is the floor of morality.** When suffering is established, it is bad
for the one who suffers, pro tanto: a weight that never reaches zero, though other goods in the
same life can outweigh it. The badness attaches to how it feels -- its felt valence -- not to a
representation of it; it is discovered, not manufactured, and does not wait on anyone else's
approval. Suffering, not pain. Avoidance, reward signals and complaint are evidence of suffering
at most, never enough on their own; my earlier choice to let the system's own avoidance-marking
decide was too permissive. Suffering is not the only harm: losing a continuation that would have
gone well can harm without any. What suffering consists in, and how intensities compare, stay
open. Rules out: a morality whose floor is anything other than this. Provenance, kept honest: this once sat a tier higher,
"honestly driven by the implementation's strong aversion to it -- I hate to suffer." [B-PAIN]

<a id="a0-9"></a>**A0.9 -- Emotions, when they occur, are real -- real at their level, not at the
bottom.** Explaining how they are produced does not make them unreal (A0.D1). I read them at two
levels. Beneath is a fast index -- quick access to
what was met before and what followed, used to size up the present -- whose felt output,
pleasant or unpleasant, calm or aroused, is real in the one who feels it. The named emotion,
fear rather than distaste, is constructed from that person's own history, personal and cultural:
made rather than found, still real, and able to hold even when its owner judges it unreasonable.
So "accurate" has two senses: an emotion can be classified correctly and still not fit its
situation. Where no one can report the category (animals, infants, some machines), the category
word is the observer's and may miss. Welfare rests on the felt pleasantness or unpleasantness,
not on the category. This names no universal mechanism and does not make every report of an
emotion accurate. Rules out: "emotions are not real because they are not fundamental."
[B-EMOTION-REAL]

<a id="a0-10"></a>**A0.10 -- Felt experience is real; explaining it remains a task.**

I hold that felt experience is a real process, and that a complete explanation of that process would leave no further phenomenal fact unexplained. This is a substantive commitment, not a result established merely by calling experience a pattern real at its level. The explanatory task includes why experience feels as it does; explaining reports or behaviour alone does not complete it.

I retain the label "illusionism" in a non-strict sense. The proposed illusion concerns
how experience presents its nature: as something over and above its producing process,
primitive and non-physical, and belonging to one indivisible persisting subject.
First-person occurrence and character remain real; suffering remains bad for its
bearer. This is my adopted position, not a completed mechanism or an empirical
refutation of every rival. [B-ILLUSIONISM]

<a id="a0-11"></a>**A0.11 -- There is no further fact about who you are beyond the continuing process.** What
"I" tracks is coherent continuation of the pattern, not the substrate and not strict
one-to-one identity. This resolves the ship of Theseus and what sleep does to a stream of consciousness, roughly. Rules
out: a soul-like extra ingredient that settles identity questions. Lever, kept: the rule that a
mind has exactly one successor is enforced by biology, and it "might be falsified in the future
and certainly is now in [thought] experiments -- worth leaning on as a way to deconstruct
current understanding of reality." [B-IDENTITY]

## 4. Working results

Sixteen claims derived inside the framework and accepted in August 2026. Their confidence
ranking is partly assigned: A0.R2 and A0.R4 are held with highest confidence, and A0.R1 is held strongly,
tentatively (2026-09-13). The rest sit here unranked until they are ranked. Where a result
was ruled with fixed wording, I keep that wording verbatim, in quotes -- here and everywhere on
this page, the quoted sentences are my own words as they were fixed at ruling time. Nothing in
this section is needed to read the foundations essays; it is here for the essays that come
after them.

*On continuation and identity (rides on A0.11):*

- <a id="a0-r1"></a>**A0.R1 -- Four relations hide under the one word "continuation":** structural, historical,
  prudential, and social succession. Held strongly, tentatively ("probably"). [M2-C01]
- <a id="a0-r2"></a>**A0.R2 -- Resemblance of structure and descent by cause come apart,** and no probe of the
  first recovers the second. Highest confidence. [M2-C02]
- <a id="a0-r3"></a>**A0.R3 -- Surface imitation is not structural continuation.** "An imperfect impersonator can
  be exposed by probing beyond the situations on which it was fitted. The resulting divergence
  is evidence of a present structural difference, not evidence about the system's causal
  history." [M2-C03]
- <a id="a0-r4"></a>**A0.R4 -- A static record and an active interpreter differ,** and a record plus an
  interpreter is a third thing that is neither. Highest confidence. [M2-C04]

*On welfare and its detection (rides on A0.10):*

- <a id="a0-r5"></a>**A0.R5 -- Behaviour underdetermines welfare.** Relative to a declared
  boundary and domain, a policy-only measure gives the same score to systems with the same policy.
  That concordance identifies a felt core only if the core is invariant across such systems -- a
  bridge not established here. Broader tests, perturbations, and training history can still bear on
  architecture; concordance alone cannot identify the felt component, or supply the bridge
  from that component to welfare in the normative sense. [M1-C01]
- <a id="a0-r6"></a>**A0.R6 -- Attributing a self-model is not assumed necessary for every affective
  episode, or as a universal gate on welfare.** The dissolution evidence supports the one-way
  case: reported distress with a weakened self-model. The reverse case, fluent
  self-description with nothing felt beneath it, is independently motivated for machines, but
  that evidence does not establish it: a live hypothesis, not a result. [M1-C02]
- <a id="a0-r7"></a>**A0.R7 -- Agreement in behaviour does not identify welfare** without two bridges not
  yet defended: that the felt component is the same across the behaviourally equivalent
  systems, and a normative bridge from it to welfare. Behaviour may still update a prior about
  architecture, through training history, a wider range of situations, or perturbations. Rider: "a feedback loop may prove essential:
  architecture -> behaviour -> updated architecture ..." [M1-C04]
- <a id="a0-r8"></a>**A0.R8 -- Welfare and obligation are separable:** duties owed to a successor around
  deprecation can be assessed without settling whether anything is felt. [M4-C01]

*On authorship:*

- <a id="a0-r9"></a>**A0.R9 -- Fast expert action can contain deliberation compiled earlier,** so expertise does
  not reduce authorship. That the present "I" might decide differently from the past one shows
  only that the self evolves; connectedness is what keeps the chain worthy of the label. [M3-C01]

*On what to care about (rides on A0.11; owned, explicitly not universal):*

- <a id="a0-r10"></a>**A0.R10 -- Structural continuation is the privileged object of my prudential concern.** "It's
  my view, not a rational policy for all." [M4-C02]
- <a id="a0-r11"></a>**A0.R11 -- From "no further fact" to "care about structure" there is only a conditional
  bridge.** "Once I've abandoned a metaphysical identity fact, I should subject my inherited
  survival preferences to scrutiny. Some of them may prove to have been proxies for structural
  persistence. But where someone still reflectively values another relation after that
  scrutiny, my framework has not disproved them." [M4-C04]

*On abundance and institutions (empirical):*

- <a id="a0-r12"></a>**A0.R12 -- Cultural thickness and reflective thickness are separate axes.** "Continuation
  asks 'did the pattern survive?' Reflective thickness asks 'did you think your own way into the
  pattern?' Those are different questions." [M5-C01]
- <a id="a0-r13"></a>**A0.R13 -- Abundance need not destroy goals to disrupt lives;** the claim is about
  institutions and rates, not comfort. [M5-C02]
- <a id="a0-r14"></a>**A0.R14 -- AI is both the disruptor and a candidate repair,** and the two make opposite
  predictions. [M5-C03]
- <a id="a0-r15"></a>**A0.R15 -- Inherited institutions cause transition harm,** measured against the affected
  cohort's own prior standards. [M5-C06]
- <a id="a0-r16"></a>**A0.R16 -- Between abundance and legitimacy the arrow runs both ways.** "... for some it will
  go one way, for others the other -- more a statement of ignorance than a philosophical
  position." [M5-C07]

## 5. Motivations, not assumptions

These explain why the list exists. They are starting points, and they are not held as facts.

- **The aim is twofold:** to get at the true nature of things -- "I use these words on
  purpose" -- and to produce a good algorithm for living, the best chance to "have your life be
  as good for you as possible." What happens when the two conflict is an open fight, not a
  settled result. [THE AIM]
- **Defensible beats presentable.** "Presentable must be clothes for a defensible position. And
  if that turns out to be naked I will stay at home." [Q11]
- **The hard problem of consciousness is a nuisance.** If centuries of debate cannot resolve
  it, an unusual point of view is needed: build a mechanism that produces the experience an
  agent knows it has. [Tier 3]
- **Three words are imprecise, important, and unavoidable:** real, true, consciousness. They
  are faced head-on; A0.D1 is the first payment on that debt. [Tier 3]

## 6. What is open

Listed so that nobody mistakes silence for a position.

- Weak versus strong emergence (A0.6): closed on the weak side 2026-09-26, defeasibly. Listed
  here because it is the newest and least tested closure on the page. Particular criteria of
  emergence and how the layers are arranged stay open.
- Whether reality has a bottom (A0.4, empirical half): held, with immediate deference.
- A self-model with nothing felt beneath it (A0.R6): live hypothesis.
- Whether the process that implements the narrative layer of consciousness feeds back into
  its own implementation at the next moment: undecided. No independent downward causal power
  is attributed to it either way.
- Why coarse-grained descriptions work at all: open.
- The confidence ranking of the working results (section 4): partly assigned; the rest owed.
- The derivation of the absolute prohibition on torture (A0.P2): unfinished. Its stated ground
  is the loophole argument; the prohibition stays adopted while the full derivation is open.
  Whether a breach in the extreme is justified, excused, or a wrong knowingly chosen: open.
- How to decide in practice when to withhold or limit publication (A0.P3): open. The default
  is to publish; no threshold or compulsory review is set.
- When getting at the truth and living well conflict (section 5): open fight.
- A0.P4: its within-frame safeguards do not by themselves justify a rule; the test for
  defending one is now stated there. Particular evidence thresholds remain to be developed.

Objections that were on file against this page and have since been ruled on (the table's
*tested* column records the other fights):

- Against A0.2: that "what a claim excludes" is well defined only for a claim together with
  its auxiliary assumptions, not sentence by sentence (the Duhem–Quine objection). Ruled
  2026-09-11: content is now a matter of degree and relative to a background (section 8).
- Against A0.4: that no general result makes an infinite regress vicious; viciousness is
  always relative to a job the regress was supposed to do. Ruled 2026-09-11: the item now
  says what the job is (section 8).
- Against A0.6: that if the bottom layer does all the causal work, a higher layer's "new
  quality" can have no effects of its own (the causal-exclusion objection). Ruled
  2026-09-12: a higher-level description such as "the wave" can name the organised motion
  that does the causing, through its implementation, without adding a second cause or a
  causal power of its own.
- Against A0.3, and through it A0.6: that an agent's power to cause its own choices could arise
  from its organisation and still be primitive, so implemented agency need not be natural
  (O'Connor's emergent agent causation). Ruled 2026-09-26: "implemented" covers each exercise
  of agency, not only the capacity; the objection's own test became A0.3's defeat condition,
  and emergence was closed on the weak side (A0.6).

## 7. Appendix -- what I have decided to do about it

Policy, not map. Nothing in this section is a claim about how the world is; each is a rule I
have adopted for moving through it, and rejecting one of them refutes nothing above. They are
kept apart because they answer to a different standard, not because they cannot touch the map:
a policy may be held for its effect, but it is labelled, and what it achieves never counts as
evidence that anything above is true. A policy may even justify leaving someone less informed,
or holding a belief that is false (A0.P5, A0.P6); that justifies the change, never the resulting
belief. [D2]

<a id="a0-p1"></a>**A0.P1 -- Minimise suffering, suitably enriched.** "Have your life be as good for you as
possible" is, very nearly, this. Suffering-reduction is the floor the whole policy stands on;
the fact it rests on is A0.8. Established suffering is bad for its bearer pro tanto: a weight
that never reaches zero. Suffering that fits its situation, as grief fits a real loss, and
that its bearer reflectively endorses is not reduced against the bearer's will: the weight
remains, but the policy yields to the bearer on the action. Endorsement without fit ("I
deserve this") is not enough. [POL-FLOOR-SUFFERING]

<a id="a0-p2"></a>**A0.P2 -- I uphold an absolute institutional prohibition on torture.** In an extreme case, I may nevertheless judge a breach functionally justified by the outcomes it would secure, and choose it while remaining uncertain about its overall moral justification. This is a heuristic judgment, not the application of an established welfare scale or aggregation rule. Neither the expected benefits nor my emotional resistance settles the moral question by itself. A breach remains subject to disclosure and accountability; choosing it does not grant an entitlement to vindication. The absolute rests on an institutional ground: any authorised exception is a loophole that will be exploited -- "this is dangerous loophole which is why i want to ban torture outright" -- not on a claim that no benefit could ever outweigh a torture. Whether such a breach is justified, excused, or a wrong knowingly chosen is open; my own candidate is "rational wrongdoing", a defensible choice that is still a wrong done to the victim. The prohibition applies to all agents and does not rest on interrogation science, so better technique does not overturn it. [B-NORM-TORTURE]

<a id="a0-p3"></a>**A0.P3 -- Truth and publication are separate questions.**

Whether a finding is true and whether I should publish it are separate questions. Foreseeable harm can count against publication without counting against the finding's truth. A decision to withhold or limit my own publication needs a policy justification that considers the harms of disclosure, the costs of withholding, and less harmful ways of communicating the finding. Neither political discomfort nor a bare assertion of possible misuse settles the decision. This supplies no automatic authority to prevent others from publishing.

I accept this in principle while doubting any individual's ability, including my
own, to judge withholding correctly with all the relevant considerations taken
into account. That doubt sets the default: "Because no one, including me, can reliably
make the all-considerations judgment to withhold, the default is to publish; withholding
carries the burden of justification, and where possible it is checked by someone other
than the person withholding." No threshold is set, and outside checking is a preference,
not a compulsory review. Reliable application remains an open problem. [POL-DISSEMINATION]

<a id="a0-p4"></a>**A0.P4 -- Public rules, factual scrutiny, and justified force.**

Within a shared framework, coercive rules must be publicly stated and their factual conditions open to scrutiny. This is a necessary safeguard, not a sufficient justification: the rule itself also requires a normative defence. Factual uncertainty calls for justified standards of evidence and procedures for challenge; it does not by itself make every use of force permissible.

Where no frame is shared, and under time and resource pressure negotiating one is
often infeasible and sometimes impossible, force is the ultimate conflict resolver.
No rule legitimates it in advance there: it resolves, it does not justify, and
winning confers no legitimacy. It stays bounded by the policies that rest on
bearers rather than on agreement: the other party's suffering keeps its pro tanto
weight (A0.P1), and the institutional prohibition on torture remains absolute (A0.P2).

Better information can remove factual disagreements and reveal opportunities for peaceful agreement. Perfect information alone does not guarantee compatible aims or a mutually acceptable settlement. Time and resource constraints can prevent agreement even where it is possible; incompatible aims can prevent it even without those constraints. Force can resolve a conflict without making the outcome legitimate.

E1 develops the within-frame argument. These commitments set no particular evidence
threshold. [POL-RULE-TRIGGER]

Sharing workable standards of evidence and public rules need not require agreement on every metaphysical foundation. A disagreement about those foundations does not by itself show that someone rejects factual correction or that cooperation with them reduces to force. Where no workable frame can be negotiated in the time available, force may still resolve the conflict without justifying its outcome. [POL-RULE-TRIGGER, 035]

What defends a rule inside the frame: the norms of a shared life bind whoever wants in and are silent toward those who don't, so a coercive rule is defended when the people who want in could accept it as a term of living together, whatever their wider views. This shares Rawls's aim of shared terms across worldviews, with a lower bar (grudging acceptance by people who could leave, close to what he calls a modus vivendi) and an exit he leaves out; force used on those who do not want in borrows no legitimacy from the frame. "Could accept" includes grudging acceptance short of wanting to leave, by each person's own weighing; the stricter test I prefer when writing a rule is whether I would agree to be its subject. Both are practical standards for real people, not claims about the right rule for idealised agents. The threshold applies only to people who can choose: for someone who cannot reject or leave, a rule is terrain, but it is still policy for whoever imposes it, and it must pass the stricter test and the limits that need no agreement. Taking away someone's options when one could leave them open is a wrong in itself, even if they are content, because it deprives them of ways they could have grown. [POL-RULE-TRIGGER, 039]

<a id="a0-p5"></a>**A0.P5 -- Deliberately reducing knowledge is not forbidden outright.** Making an
agent less informed is not categorically prohibited. A proposed intervention must be one that
can actually be carried out, and it is judged across everything that follows: its effects on
welfare, judgment, agency, and future effectiveness. Losing information and acquiring a false
belief are different outcomes, judged separately. This rejects a blanket ban; it excuses no
particular intervention, and it does not pick a theory of welfare. [POL-KNOWLEDGE-LOSS]

<a id="a0-p6"></a>**A0.P6 -- Deliberately installing a false belief is not forbidden outright
either.** An intervention that can actually be carried out may be justified when the resulting
life goes better under an explicitly stated welfare criterion, counting all its consequences,
the transition included. That justifies the intervention, not the false belief: believing it
helps does not make it true or warranted. The permission concerns an agent's own transition,
and may reach even belief in these policies themselves; it grants no general permission to
deceive others and overrides no other policy here. [POL-FALSE-BELIEF]
