*Builds on [the Assumptions](/assumptions) and [Essay 01](/objective-truth):
where a text came from is evidence about it, not a complete substitute for looking at the work itself.*

You write a rough paragraph about something you have thought about for years.
You know what you mean. Getting it into words is the difficult part, so you ask
an AI to make it clearer. It returns a paragraph you recognise as your position,
expressed more precisely than you managed alone.

Now the result carries a detectable mark of Claude's involvement. What has that
established about the paragraph?

Anthropic is introducing text watermarks to meet EU transparency requirements.
Its own explanation is careful about their meaning: a detected watermark can
indicate that Claude was involved, but cannot distinguish original writing from
heavy editing. It does not assign authorship.[^mark] The mark gives us evidence
of participation. The contributions still need to be examined.

**AI can help produce the words while the speaker stays the same.** The speaker
is the one whose ideas you are hearing. When the AI changes only the expression,
it is an editor. It can also develop an argument or an explanatory idea that the
person did not develop. For that contribution it is an author, and a second
speaker enters. The same AI can do both in one passage. Neither role is captured
by putting the whole result in a box labelled “AI did it”.

## 1. What changed in the paragraph?

Take this small example, invented to make the contributions visible:

> I know what I want to say. Getting it into words is difficult. Having AI help
> with that doesn't suddenly make the opinion someone else's.

An AI might rewrite it:

> Difficulty expressing a conviction does not make it less yours. Help finding
> the words need not change who is speaking.

The second version makes editorial choices. It selects words, compresses three
sentences into two, and gives the point a different rhythm. Those choices
contribute to what the reader encounters. The person supplied the position;
the AI supplied this expression of it.[^example]

That happened with this essay. I recognised my position in wording that was
foreign to me, and polished. I would credit that help, and I would still call
the position mine. The paragraph may sound different from something I would have
written unaided. That is a change in its voice, meaning its recognisable style.
It does not show that someone else now holds the position.

This becomes harder when the AI does more than rewrite.

## 2. When the AI contributes an idea

Extend the invented example. I have supplied the position that accepting help
with expression does not, by itself, change who is speaking. Now the AI adds
this argument:

> If accepting an argument made you its author, every reader persuaded by an
> argument in a book would become that argument's author. Acceptance cannot,
> by itself, tell us who developed the argument.

That adds a reason the first paragraph did not contain, and a familiar
reader-and-book framing for grasping it. In the example, the AI developed that
addition. The person can examine and accept it, and still credit the AI for
it.[^addition]

Credit for the new contribution belongs where the work was done. If the AI
developed it, the AI deserves that credit. My acceptance does not make me its
author. I may be the one who brings it to an audience, the megaphone. But once
the AI contributes ideas rather than only shaping them precisely, we are both
speakers.[^credit]

I am still the one addressing the audience. Calling the AI a second speaker
identifies whose ideas they are hearing in that contribution. Whether I agree
with those ideas, and how well I understand them, are further questions.

The sources deserve credit too. I supplied positions the argument builds on.
Other people may have supplied evidence or ideas it uses. Those contributions
remain theirs. Credit for a new step belongs to whoever developed it, and that
step can have contributors too.[^sources]

The AI brings me a philosopher's existing objection, names its source, and
presents it faithfully. I owe the philosopher credit for the argument and the AI
credit for finding and selecting it. I would not call a search engine a second
speaker because it found an argument I had never seen. AI can serve that
function too, bringing existing ideas into the discussion from what it learned
in training. An argument being new to me does not show that the AI developed it.

If the AI develops a synthesis or an explanatory framing of its own, that
contribution earns its own credit. The ideas it builds on retain theirs.

Here is the strongest objection to the comfortable phrase “AI helped me edit”:
it can conceal exactly this difference. Perhaps the AI supplied the argument
and the human merely accepted it. A byline and an assurance that the final
text was reviewed cannot show who developed the argument. That objection is
fair. The answer should include the contribution itself, rather than describe
all assistance as editing.[^objection]

I want AI credit to become ordinary. For this article, that means an overall
acknowledgement and specific attribution where AI developed a substantive
argument or explanatory idea. A label on the whole text, human-written or
AI-written, tells the reader less than saying who did which work.

For now the AI speaks alongside me, through my audience and in my name. At some
point an AI may address an audience in its own name.[^identity] Then I would not
attach myself to its positions simply because mine contributed to them. I would
still expect to be named as a source. The arrangement considered here is the one
where I bring the work to an audience.

## 3. What I can stand behind

So far, voice has meant recognisable style: does the paragraph sound like me?
There is another question: does it express something I can stand behind?

Being the person addressing an audience is different from having authored every
argument presented to it. It is also different from agreeing with those
arguments. Someone can convey a position they reject.

For positions I present as my own, I require that I can intelligently subscribe
to what is being claimed. Strictly, that means I could rebuild the argument
myself. For most things a weaker test is enough: I can restate it in my own
words and say what would make it false. Revisions can cross that boundary. If the AI's
improvements carry me into claims I can no longer intelligently subscribe to,
I would no longer call the result my voice in that sense.

Presenting a rejected position as something I advocate is a role I personally
could not take on.[^endorsement]

That standard is for positions I present as mine. Accepting someone else's
result is a different act, and it does not require complete understanding first.
Take an argument proved in Lean, a system that checks formal mathematical
proofs. A valid checked proof can warrant accepting the formal result from its
stated assumptions. I still need an explanation to understand the
argument.[^lean]

As I spend attention on the proof, I may understand more of what has been proved:
how the parts fit, why a step matters, what the conclusion permits me to say.
I subscribe more intelligently as I understand more. That growing understanding
does not transfer the proof's authorship to me.

An explanation has a receiver. It shows me how a result is built from simpler
things I already understand. A formal check can succeed without doing that.

And developing that explanation can be substantial authorship. Digesting a dense
proof, finding a framing I can follow, choosing an example that makes a step
visible—those can involve a great deal of work. The proof's existing contributions
retain their credit. The explainer earns credit for the structure they actually
develop to make it intelligible to me. As its receiver, I may find that contribution
especially valuable.

Say I develop a complete proof, and the AI develops an analogy that makes one
difficult step clear. It adds no premise and changes no conclusion. The proof is
still mine, but the explanatory idea is its contribution. For that idea, the AI
is a second speaker. Rewording my sentences and developing a way to explain the
proof are different kinds of work.[^explanation]

## 4. Wanting this human's poetry

There is another reason the source can matter, and it does not depend on a
claim that AI produces worse work.

Say two poems move me equally, one AI-written, one human-written. I judge them equally. In that comparison I do not care about their provenance. Someone else
may care very much: they want human poetry, perhaps the poetry of one particular
human. I think that preference is fair.

Tell that reader that an AI could write something as good, or even better. The
claim may grant everything about the output and still leave their preference
untouched. They wanted this human's poetry. A different source does not turn
into that human by writing a good poem.

This is where “more effective” becomes an awkward description. Effective at
what: moving me, expressing something I recognise, or giving this reader the
human poetry they wanted? Those are different questions, and they can have
different answers.[^aim] What has a claim of greater effectiveness answered, if
it leaves what this reader wanted untouched?

I do not need to share that preference to respect it. I can judge the two
poems equally and accept that another reader wants something the comparison
does not measure.

For art, the voice is what that reader wants, and voice is exactly what AI
help can change while the speaker stays the same. So a poet who runs their
drafts through an AI polish owes the reader that information.

## 5. More ways to say what you mean

Someone can describe an object they want to make, but cannot build a 3D model of
it. A generative tool can turn that description into a starting model they can
examine and develop.[^making] They have another way to explore what they meant.
The tool's contribution deserves credit, and the person's contribution can be
assessed by what they actually supplied, chose and changed.

Someone with a position to articulate, an image to develop or a form to explore
can get help bringing it into a medium they could not previously handle. Skilled
execution can carry a great deal of authorship. Help with it can also give
someone else more room to contribute. If AI supplies words or a model, that does
not by itself show that the person has nothing left to say. They may still
supply the position, choose what they want to express, develop it through the
collaboration and stand behind the result. This essay's polished wording is one
example of that possibility.

For someone paid to produce those models, that same development is a threat. So
is the automation of editorial work. I expect the professional 3D asset designer
and the editor to be gone as jobs. The capability is already there, and it will
improve massively in 2027, barring war or a ban on AI. What remains is harness
and integration: the software and workflows that put a model to work on real
tasks. Easier expression for others does not pay an editor's lost wages.[^jobs]

## 6. Read the marked paragraph again

Return to the paragraph from the beginning. Before deciding that AI's
participation has settled its authorship, look at the work.

In the two-sentence revision, the person supplied the position and the AI the
wording. The AI was an editor, and the speaker stayed the same. Its further
argument about readers added a reason and an explanatory framing. For those
contributions it was an author and a second speaker. The person's acceptance
did not transfer that work to them.

For the person making the 3D model, ask the same questions: what did they
describe, choose and change, and what did the tool supply?

If the AI brought in an existing argument, credit its source and the AI's work
in finding it. Being shown an idea for the first time does not tell you who
developed it.

This essay includes both kinds of work, editorial and authorial. Its byline and
notes name the arguments and framing the AI developed. On those, the essay has
more than one speaker. And if what you wanted was a particular human's poetry,
you can still say so. An account of the contributions lets you make that choice
with more information than a single mark provides.
